C1 for trainee paramedics: a step-by-step guide

Everything you need to go from a car licence to C1, in the order you'll do it.

By Stuart Abbott, former DVSA Vocational Examiner · Last checked against GOV.UK: October 2026 · All resources

Many ambulance trusts ask trainee paramedics and ambulance technicians to hold C1, or at least provisional C1, on their driving licence. It can feel like a lot of paperwork on top of your studies, so here's the whole route in plain English.

  1. Check what your trust actually asks for

    Requirements vary. Some trusts want a full C1 licence before you start, some want provisional C1, and some train new staff to drive after you join. The person specification for the role will say. NHS Health Careers explains more.

  2. Check your licence

    If you passed your car test before 1 January 1997, your licence probably already includes C1, which covers vehicles from 3,500kg up to 7,500kg. You can see your categories on the back of your photocard or by using View your driving licence on GOV.UK. If you passed after that date, you'll only have category B, so carry on below.

  3. Get your medical and provisional licence

    You'll need a D4 medical examination report, completed by a doctor, and a D2 application form. Send both to DVLA to add provisional C1 to your licence. There's no application fee, but doctors usually charge for the medical. GOV.UK says to allow about 3 weeks. See applying for a provisional lorry or bus licence.

  4. Pass the theory test

    The theory test has two parts: 100 multiple-choice questions (pass mark 85) and a hazard perception test of 19 video clips (pass mark 67 out of 100). You can book your theory test on GOV.UK. Your trust will tell you whether you also need the Driver CPC parts that professional drivers take.

  5. Train in a C1 vehicle

    This is where I come in. Most trainee paramedics don't have a 7.5 tonne lorry to practise in, so I can arrange hire of a C1 vehicle that meets DVSA's test rules and train you in it from your first drive to test day. As a former DVSA Vocational Examiner, I'll focus on exactly what the test looks for.

  6. Pass the off-road reversing test

    This part lasts up to 30 minutes and includes an S-shaped reverse into a bay. Once you pass, you have 6 months to pass the on-road test. See the off-road test on GOV.UK.

  7. Pass the on-road driving test

    About an hour of driving, including vehicle safety questions and around 10 minutes of independent driving. You can make up to 12 driving faults, with no serious or dangerous faults. See the on-road test on GOV.UK.
