C1 for ambulance crews

Training to be a paramedic or ambulance technician?

Many ambulance trusts ask for C1, or provisional C1, on your licence because frontline ambulances can weigh more than 3.5 tonnes. Requirements vary between trusts, so check the person specification for the role you're applying for. NHS Health Careers explains more.

I'll train you for C1 from provisional licence to test day, in a hired vehicle that meets DVSA's test rules, so you don't need a lorry of your own.

C1

What you need to know

  • Passed your car test before 1 January 1997? Your licence probably already covers C1, up to 7.5 tonnes. Check the categories on your photocard first.
  • If not, you'll need a D4 medical and a D2 application for provisional entitlement, then the theory and hazard perception tests before your practical test.
  • The vehicle: training and your test take place in a hired C1 vehicle that meets DVSA's test rules, including a closed box body at least as wide and high as the cab. Hire cost is extra.
  • Got access to a suitable vehicle already? We can use it, as long as it meets the test rules, has L-plates and is insured for a learner driver.

FAQ

Common questions

Which categories do you train?

C1, D1, C, D, C+E and D+E. Tell me the category your candidate is training for when you get in touch.

How much does training cost?

Every booking is quoted individually. Driving schools, tell me the category, test date and number of training days you need. Individual C1 and D1 drivers, tell me what you drive now and what you need to drive. I'll come back to you with a clear quote.

Do I need C1 to become a paramedic?

Often, but not always. Many ambulance trusts ask for C1 or provisional C1, while others provide driver training after you join. Check the person specification for the role, and NHS Health Careers has more detail. If you need C1, I can train you in a hired test vehicle. More about C1 for ambulance crews.

Can I book C1 or D1 training myself?

Yes. I train individual drivers for C1, including trainee paramedics, in a hired test vehicle, and D1 minibus drivers. Tell me what you drive now and what you need to drive, and I'll explain the steps.
